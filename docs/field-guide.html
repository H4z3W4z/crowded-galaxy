CROWDED
GALAXY

The galaxy is too small for everyone.

A fast game of area control where you lead a succession of alien civilizations — each one rises across the spiral, overreaches, and collapses into a haunting Remnant while you launch the next. This guide explains every piece on the board.

2–5 players · 12 rounds · ~25 min

The rhythm of a game

Every civilization you lead travels the same arc. Learning to feel where you are on it — and when to let go — is the whole game.

The spiral

The galaxy is four spiral arms wound around a dense, heavily defended core. New civilizations enter at the Rim Gates on the outer frontier and push inward.

The core holds most of the Relics and is the contested prize — but the arms are corridors, so reaching it means fighting through chokepoints. Wormholes (the magenta arcs) are the only fast way across the galaxy, which makes the systems they touch worth fighting for.

Arm tips are near dead-ends — one neighbor and one wormhole. Easy to defend, slow to escape.

Reading the board

Every marker on a system tells you something. Swatches below are drawn exactly as they appear in the game.

Planet types

Each system holds one to three planets. Every species favors one type and scores +1 Influence from each system it controls containing at least one favored planet — so where your species thrives shapes where you expand.

A turn, step by step

On your turn you either Expand your civilization, or Collapse it. An Expand turn runs in four beats:

1 · Recall

Gather your strength

Pull spare population off your systems back into your hand (leave at least one token everywhere you want to keep). An emptied system is abandoned.

2 · Conquer

Take new systems

Spend tokens from your hand to seize systems adjacent to your empire — one at a time. A brand-new civilization must make its first conquest at a Rim Gate. The cost of a system is:

cost = 2 + defenders + Starbases + Hazard
3 · Redeploy

Spread out or dig in

Redistribute your surviving population across the systems you hold, at least one token each. Thin borders are cheap to attack; deep stacks are hard to crack.

4 · Score

Count your Influence

+1 for every system you hold, +1 more for each favored-habitat system, plus whatever your species, trait, and Remnants add. Influence is victory — and also the currency you spend to draft better civilizations.

The reinforcement die

On your final conquest of a turn, if you're short by one to three tokens, you may gamble: roll the die and add its result to what you committed. Land enough and the system falls; come up short and the assault fails. Either way, your conquering ends there.

Faces are 0, 0, 0, 1, 2, 3 — a coin-flip on +1 or better, and a one-in-six shot at a full +3.

Death & rebirth

No empire lasts. As you spread, you run out of population to defend what you hold — and that's the moment to Collapse.

A collapsed civilization leaves one token in each system as a Remnant Empire: weaker, frozen in place, but still scoring Influence and still shaping the board. Next turn you draft an entirely new species-and-trait combination and begin again — while your old empires linger, haunting the galaxy.

Squeeze one more scoring turn, or collapse now and come back fresh? That decision is the heart of the game.

Drafting a civilization

Six Species + Trait combinations sit in a row. The top one is free; reaching further down costs 1 Influence per combination you skip — placed on the ones you pass, where a later player can scoop it up. You begin the game with 5 Influence, so the market is a real choice from the very first turn.

◇ WORMHOLE-SAVVY+3 pop
The Trait — a random special power. Wormhole conquests cost one less; you may redeploy through wormholes.
◉ PELAGIC ORACLES5 pop
The Species — a favored habitat plus an active power and a Remnant power. Ocean habitat; converts lone enemy tokens; its Remnant scores beside opponents.

Twelve species, twenty traits — the pairings are random, which is where the game's best moments come from: a ridiculous, promising combination you'll ride until it burns out.
